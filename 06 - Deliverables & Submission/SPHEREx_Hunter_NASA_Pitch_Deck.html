Slide 2 of 7 • The Challenge

Finding Needles in an Infrared Haystack

1. Petabyte Firehose

SPHEREx maps the entire sky every 6 months across 102 narrow near-IR bands (0.75 - 5.0 µm), observing >1 billion objects per epoch. Each raw FITS exposure is 62 MB.

2. Glacial Motion

Planet X (300-800 AU) crawls at <0.1"/yr (<0.5 px per survey). Cold brown dwarfs (T < 300 K) emit zero visible light, making them 100% invisible to optical telescopes like Gaia and Hubble.

3. False Alarm Overload

Automated difference pipelines drown in false positives from diffraction spikes and bad pixels. Meanwhile, ordinary citizens are locked out by complex astronomical software.

Slide 4 of 7 • Scientific Rigor

ZOGY Fourier Differencing & Gaia Astrometry

ZOGY Subtraction

Optimal Fourier-space differencing: stationary background stars subtract to neutral flat gray (128 mid-gray), leaving moving objects as high-contrast white & black dipole pairs.

S_corr = (D - R*P) / √Var
Sub-Pixel Astrometry

Gnomonic Tangent (TAN) WCS projection maintains 0.18" astrometric RMS (0.03 native detector pixels) across 26 years, preventing false alignment residuals.

RMS < 0.18" (0.03 px)
Gaia Optical Vetting

Live cone search against 1.8 billion ESA Gaia DR3 stars. If an infrared heat source has No Gaia Match, it confirms an ultra-cold optical dropout (Planet X / Y-dwarf)!

Slide 5 of 7 • Chemical Diagnostics

102-Band SPHEREx Infrared Spectroscopy

Beyond Images: Full SED Chemistry

SPHEREx is an all-sky spectrophotometer. Our platform maps the full Spectral Energy Distribution from 0.75 µm to 5.00 µm across Arrays A1–A6.

Array Signal Co-Addition: Combines multi-channel signals to boost signal-to-noise ratio by >4×, recovering faint outer solar system candidates.

Horizontal Zoom & Pan: Sideways mouse wheel zoom up to 10× to examine fine molecular absorption wings and continuum variations.
